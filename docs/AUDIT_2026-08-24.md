### 5. MEDIUM — DBL/PFL coverage watch is legally wrong in three independent ways

**5a. Wrong statutory test.** `src/lib/coverage.ts` averages hours over 26 weeks.
WCL § 202(2) is verbatim: "an employer shall become a covered employer from and
after the expiration of four weeks following the employment of one or more personal
or domestic employees who **work for a minimum of twenty hours per week** for such
employer **and are employed on each of at least thirty days in any calendar year**."
That is a per-week test with no averaging window, plus a 30-day condition, attaching
four weeks later. The "26 consecutive weeks" figure comes from WCL § 203 and
12 NYCRR 380-2.5, which govern when an *employee becomes eligible to claim benefits*
from an already-covered employer — a different question. The only averaging method NY
publishes is **8 weeks**, on page 2 of Form PFL-Waiver.

The 26-week average fails in the direction where penalties live: 8 summer weeks at
40 hrs plus 18 weeks at 4 hrs averages 15.1 hrs/wk, so the app says "not covered,"
but under § 202(2) the household became covered four weeks into the 40-hour stretch.

**5b. The 175-day trigger is both the wrong rule and unreachable.** It is an
employee-eligibility / waiver condition, not a coverage trigger. Worse, the only
caller (`AdminDashboard.tsx:106-109`) selects `'pay_date, hours_worked'` and casts
`as Paystub[]`, so `stub.daily_hours` is `undefined` on every row and
`coverage.ts:53` always falls to the one-day-per-stub proxy. With weekly stubs the
proxy caps near 52, so a threshold of 175 is mathematically unreachable. The
`as Paystub[]` cast is what suppressed the type error. `daily_hours` **is** fully
populated on all 14 live stubs, so real data is being discarded: true days worked is
**26**, the app computes **14**.

**5c. Fixed `/26` divisor understates hours/week whenever tenure < 26 weeks.**
`coverage.ts:41` divides by a constant 26. Live: 117 hrs → app displays **4.5
hrs/wk**; true is **7.8–9.0**. At 25 hrs/wk a genuinely-covered employee would show
"ok" for ~19 weeks and never show "required" until ~24 weeks.

**The money is correct.** SDI $0 and PFL $0 on all 14 stubs is right, and no
incorrect deduction has ever been taken from her pay — she is genuinely under
20 hrs/wk by every measure. This is a warning-system defect, not a payroll defect.
Had 5b flipped `pfl_covered` on spuriously it would have produced an unauthorized
~$11/yr wage deduction (Labor Law § 193 exposure).

Related corrections: the PFL waiver condition is "<20 hrs/wk **and** won't work 175
days in 52 weeks," not <20 hrs alone; and a household whose only worker is under
20 hrs/wk is not a covered employer, so the waiver is legally inapplicable rather
than required (still worth collecting defensively). Workers' comp is separately not
required at 9 hrs/wk (WCL § 3(1) Group 12 sets 40 hrs/wk).

Source conflict resolved: NY DOL P712 and tax.ny.gov's "Hiring Household Help"
(updated 2026-08-10) both say **40** hours for disability coverage. Resolved to
**20** — WCB administers Article 9 (DOL and DTF do not) and WCL § 202(2) is
unambiguous. The 40-hour figure is bleed-through from § 202(3) and § 3(1) Group 12.
Immaterial at 9 hrs/wk, which is below both.

**FIXED 2026-08-24.**

### 6. MEDIUM — Sick-leave gaps that unlimited PTO does not close

The unlimited-unpaid-PTO policy is documented in `CLAUDE.md` and is **accurate and
compliant on hours**: it exceeds the 40-hour § 196-b(1)(a) floor for an employer
with four or fewer employees and net income ≤ $1M, and unpaid is the correct tier.
Accrual tracking is therefore **ACCEPTED as not required** (owner decision
2026-08-24) and no accrual calculation will be added.

Three items survive that unlimited *unpaid* PTO does not cover. 6c is fixed; 6a and
6b are **OPEN pending an owner decision**, since both add behaviour rather than
correcting an error.

**6a. Paid Prenatal Leave is entirely absent.** `grep -rni prenatal src/ docs/
supabase/ CLAUDE.md` → **0 hits**. It is codified in § 196-b itself (subdivision
4-a), the same statute the policy document claims compliance with.

**Eligibility settled 2026-08-24 (owner asked whether a 9 hr/wk employee
qualifies): yes, she does.** Part-time status and hours worked are both irrelevant
to this entitlement. Primary sources:

- **NY DOL Form P695** (Paid Prenatal Leave Law, Employee Fact Sheet, rev. 12-10-24),
  verbatim: *"All full time and part time private sector employees, including
  employees who are overtime exempt, are entitled to 20 hours of Paid Prenatal Leave
  per year."* And under the heading **NO MINIMUM WORK REQUIREMENT**: *"You do not
  need to work a minimum number of hours to earn access to Paid Prenatal Leave. You
  are entitled to 20 hours of Paid Prenatal Leave per 52-week period. Newly hired
  employees are entitled to 20 hours of paid prenatal leave as soon as they are
  hired."*
- The same fact sheet defines covered employers as *"people or businesses, outside
  of government, that employ others … persons, corporations, limited liability
  companies, or associations in any occupation, industry, trade, business, or
  service."* A household employer is a **person who employs another**, so it is in
  scope. Only federal/state/local government employers are excluded.
- **Labor Law § 196-b(4-a)** reads *"every employer shall be required to provide to
  its employees twenty hours of paid prenatal personal leave."* "Every employer",
  with no reference to the employer-size tiers in subdivision 1, no hours threshold,
  and no carve-out for domestic or household workers anywhere in § 196-b. NY DOL
  separately confirms domestic workers are covered by § 196-b generally.

Mechanics that differ from the sick-leave model and matter for implementation:

| | Paid Prenatal Leave |
|---|---|
| Amount | 20 hours, **paid at the regular rate** ($22.00/hr → **$440** max per period) |
| Accrual | None. Front-loaded in full, available from the first day of employment |
| Period | **Rolling 52 weeks from first use**, NOT the calendar year. Unused hours do **not** carry over |
| Increments | Must be usable in **hourly** increments |
| Scope | Only the pregnant employee (not a partner attending appointments). Covers exams, procedures, monitoring, testing, provider discussions, end-of-pregnancy care, and fertility treatment. Not postnatal care |
| Documentation | Employer **may not** request health details or medical records |
| Separation | No payout of unused hours |
| Interaction | A **separate** benefit from § 196-b sick leave, not drawn from the same bank |

The unlimited unpaid PTO policy therefore does **not** discharge this: the
entitlement is to *paid* time. Exposure is contingent (it only arises if she becomes
pregnant) and capped at $440 per 52-week period, so this is a readiness gap rather
than a present liability. Prenatal leave hours are **wages**, so they are subject to
FICA and belong in gross pay and W-2 Box 1.

One limit on the research: no NY DOL guidance was found that names household or
domestic employers explicitly in the prenatal-leave context. The conclusion rests on
the statute's "every employer" language, the absence of any exclusion, the P695
definition of a covered employer as a "person … that employ[s] others", and DOL's
confirmation that domestic workers are covered by § 196-b.

"every dollar the admin needs to set aside for taxes per stub."

RSF is a real, separately-assessed NY liability: NYS-45 line 5 = line 3 × 0.075%,
and NYS-45-I states it "cannot be used as a credit for the Federal Unemployment Tax
Act (FUTA)," so there is no federal recovery.

```
Obligations 2026 to date
  Federal (4 quarterly 1040-ES)    409.38   (FICA 393.92 + FUTA 15.46)
  NY      (NYS-45 total_ui_due)    105.52   (UI 103.60 + RSF 1.94)
                                  --------
                                   514.90
Reserved (deposit_paystub)         512.98
                                  --------
Shortfall                            1.92   = RSF 1.94 owed − FUTA 0.02 over-accrual
```

Confirmed by the ledger: `withdrawal_filing` totals −$131.49 = $52.76 (NYS-45 Q2,
which **includes** RSF) + $78.73 (1040-ES Q2). The reserve pays out more per NYS-45
than it takes in. About $7.73/yr at a full wage year — small, but a systematic
one-directional leak in the number presented as the tax-safety balance, and it grows
with wages. **RSF itself is being computed and paid correctly on the NYS-45**; only
the per-stub reserve is short.

**FIXED 2026-08-24.**
